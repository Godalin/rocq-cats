# Graphs, free categories and limit shapes

A graph has vertices and a setoid of edges for each pair of endpoints.
Graph morphisms preserve endpoints and respect the chosen edge equality.
Paths are generated by identities, edges and composition. The free
category equips them with the congruence for edge equality and the
category laws. Interpreting generating edges in a category extends to
an interpretation of every path.

## Cones on generators

Naturality on the generating edges suffices for a cone over a free
diagram. The limit construction uses products indexed by vertices and
edges and an equalizer of the two endpoint maps. Finite generating data
can supply those products even when the free category has infinitely many
paths. [Free diagrams](../../theories/Cat/Diagram/Free.v) · [Graph limits](../../theories/Cat/Limit/Graph/Core.v) · [Finite generators](../../theories/Cat/Limit/Graph/Finite.v)

For a presented shape with relations, the diagram must respect those
relations. A suitable indexing functor lets one compare restrictions and
extensions of cones. The comparison is natural in the vertex, so it
transfers the universal property. [Cone reindexing](../../theories/Cat/Limit/Reindex.v) · [Cone presheaves](../../theories/Cat/Diagram/Presheaf.v) · [Generator diagrams](../../theories/Cat/Diagram/Generator.v)

## A natural cone comparison for nonfree shapes

[Diagram.Presheaf](../../theories/Cat/Diagram/Presheaf.v) makes that transfer
explicit as `cone_reindex_nat_iso HF HQ D`. It compares the cone presheaves
of `D` and `D ∘F Q`, naturally in the apex. The fixed-apex component is
`cone_reindex_iso HF HQ D X`; see [Limits](Limits.md) for size bounds and
the `generated_cone_nat_iso` specialization to finite generators.

The [cone interface regressions](../../tests/category/Cones.v) include a finite free
shape and the nonfree `SquareShape`. For the square, `SquareEvaluation`
evaluates paths in `UnderlyingGraph SquareShape`. It is full because every
arrow has a one-edge preimage, and essentially surjective because its object
map is the identity. The upper/right and left/lower routes remain inequivalent
free paths, as witnessed by counting the top edge, but their images agree by
`square_comm`. The cone comparison applies despite this failure of faithfulness.

## Free diagrams and comma-initiality

For a fixed graph `G` and target category `C`,
[GenDiagramCat](../../theories/Cat/Diagram/Generator.v) has graph
interpretations as objects and generator-natural vertex arrows as morphisms.
Its hom equality compares those arrows pointwise. It does not compare vertex
functions or commuting-square witnesses by Rocq equality.

Restriction gives a functor from `FctCat (FreeCat G) C` to this category.
`GenUnit` has the direct universal-arrow property, proved with the
existing `FreeN` path-extension theorem. `GenFreeObj` packages it
as initiality in a structured-arrow category. The general `FreeAdj`
then constructs `GenFreeAdj`; its unit and counit are invertible,
yielding `generator_diagram_equiv`.

This is an ordinary adjunction between two categories of diagrams, with all
natural transformations retained as morphisms. It is the selected alternative
case study for #34, not a global `FreeCat ⊣ UnderlyingGraph` in `CatCat`.
The latter's equality remains natural isomorphism. No new strict category of
all categories or graphs is introduced; that distinct ambient construction
would need its own object-map equality and transport design.
